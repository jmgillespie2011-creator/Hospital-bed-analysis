Validation

A model is only useful if it reproduces what actually happened. These checks compare the model with observed data, including years it was not calibrated to. Failures are shown as plainly as successes.

The most important finding. No single version of the model reproduces both 2015/16 and 2024/25. Tuned to today, the model predicts far more long waits in 2015/16 than actually happened. Tuned to 2015/16, it predicts far fewer long waits today than actually happen. In other words, hospitals now produce much longer waits at the same average occupancy than they did a decade ago, which matches NHS England's own caution that hospitals now experience pressure at lower occupancy. Possible reasons include less flexible use of beds (post-pandemic infection control, more single-sex and specialty ring-fencing), more variable demand, and changes in how waits are recorded. This is why the simulator shows both a "today's system" and a "more flexible system": occupancy results barely differ between them, but waits differ a lot, and the truth for any future scenario is likely to lie between them.

Checks

What this means for using the simulator

  • Occupancy estimates are robust: they follow mostly from the arithmetic of demand and beds, and both versions of the model agree.
  • Waits for a bed are sensitive to how flexibly beds can be used. Treat the two systems as a range, not a precise forecast.
  • Direction and size of changes (scenario minus baseline) are more reliable than absolute levels, because most model errors affect the baseline and the scenario alike.
